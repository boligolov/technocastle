*My friend, I'll play this game with you,*

With dead, unblinking eyes, Alice watched a heavy night cloud lumber toward the full Moon, about to blot it out with its bulk. Couldn't be helped: a horror game had to have moonlight. Before her, right in the middle of the city of the dead, stood an old mansion. The floors had rotted through, the roof had sagged, and beneath the house, in an enormous basement, a revolting boss monster lay in wait. According to the lore, it was made up of the former owners of this once-beautiful home, all mutated into a single creature.

*Well, today I'm an NPC[*](#0x00-npc),* the girl thought.

On the outskirts of the city, a battle was raging. Four brave souls, dispensing eternal rest with fire and assorted sharp implements, were fighting their way through hordes of the walking dead toward the three-story miniature palace. Their goal: defeat the basement boss and chalk up another level. The client was down there, somewhere among the squad[*](#0x00-squad) of well-equipped virtual fighters. Alice made out the four tiny red figures and silently thanked the developers for the wallhack[*](#0x00-wallhack) built into the monsters' vision. Thanks to it, every creature on the map always knew where the players were, seeing them through obstacles at any distance. Grossly unfair from a human point of view, but it made life much easier for the game's artificial intelligence, whose job was to entertain the players with mass attacks and the advertised unconventional behavior of its mobs[*](#0x00-mob).

Alice had possessed a Witch—a mutated zombie woman, the character[*](#0x00-pers) with the widest arsenal of attacks and movements. Her spawn point[*](#0x00-spawn), however, was a disappointment: much too far from the client. She would have to spend some time getting closer, see who was who and what they were carrying, and only then work out how to kill them.

The simplest solution would be to overwhelm them by sheer numbers, charging in with the rest of the zombies, making physical contact, and getting the job done. But a coordinated team with good guns might not give her a single chance. And this was probably a good team. The client was a wealthy man who could afford to hire any professional he wanted to escort him through a game level.

Alice looked around and headed for the exit through what had once been a beautiful garden surrounding the mansion, getting a feel for the body of the local restless dead as she went.

Her lower jaw dangled from scraps of skin. Through the torn abdominal muscles, intestines that had not yet rotted were visible. Studying her hands, which had suddenly sprouted claws, she thought of lazy game designers and slashed budgets. Standard monsters, standard zombies, standard people mutated by a standard virus.

Such is the hard and thankless life of the ordinary undead!

In the nighttime silence, broken by distant bursts of gunfire, came a slightly louder bang: a grenade had exploded. The self-learning game AI was throwing wave after wave of freshly generated undead into the fight. The four players were having the time of their lives, unaware that an invisible, extra—fifth—player had appeared on the map.

The Agency's hackers had smuggled Alice onto the client's private server, which he maintained for his virtual amusements, and managed to activate some of the debugging mechanisms. Unfortunately, they hadn't been able to get hold of the developer interface, but Alice could choose any monster she liked and possess it. There was one catch: she could only switch after dying, which was never pleasant. Get too caught up in the game, start believing what was happening, accepting it as reality—and you would die for real.

The girl broke into a run.

For dead flesh, her Witch had remarkably good physical abilities. Both her speed and her jumping range far exceeded anything human. In a single movement, Alice reached the roof of the nearest building and set off toward the four plunderers of the city of the dead, leaping from rooftop to rooftop.

The Witch landed soundlessly on the broken tiles. The developers hadn't added any material sounds for the roof of this particular building; evidently, they hadn't expected monsters to jump from it. A surprise attack from above was a fine way to make players shit bricks[*](#0x00-shitbricks), but apparently that would have to happen somewhere else. Closer to the mansion, most likely.

Below, on the cobbled street, four men were shooting back at the dead pressing in from every side. Muzzle flashes lit the alley. Spent cartridges bounced and clinked over the stones, while the flesh of yet another torn-apart monster splattered black stains across the walls and pavement.

&mdash; Dumb as rocks, aren't they? — came a man's voice. — You said "smart AI," "self-learning." They can't even attack properly!

&mdash; Give it time. It'll show us yet, — a second man replied placidly. — It'll study us, then give us hell. We've only just started.

The local restless dead came in about a dozen models, and for variety the developers changed their clothes. But each monster type had the same moveset[*](#0x00-moveset), and every one of those movesets was depressingly pathetic.

So. Which player was the client? The Witch's vision showed her their health bars[*](#0x00-health), but not their nicknames. Their health had dipped a little, but nothing catastrophic. Since they weren't healing[*](#0x00-heal), they were clearly saving their medkits. Which meant their supply wasn't unlimited. For the time being, though, the boys were having a nice little stroll.

Alice retreated from the edge of the roof into the darkness and followed the players.

The tank[*](#0x00-tank) was easy to identify: the tallest of them, encased in metal knight's armor, with a heavy machine gun in his hands. The knight's armor, already liberally splashed with black blood, gleamed with bare metal and was covered in painted Christian crosses, like protective charms. He moved slowly, but that was his job: lead the way, draw the monsters' attention, and shoot anything that fancied a bite of fresh human flesh. A sword scabbard swung against his leg with every step. Excellent for close combat against squishy zombies when the ammunition ran out. And definitely not the client. The client would want his creature comforts.

The second character, judging by the pouches marked with red crosses, was the medic. In addition to his medkits, he carried a shotgun, a pistol, two hatchets on his belt, and an assortment of grenades. Support[*](#0x00-support). A good class, but wrong again.

That left two assault troopers with roughly identical loadouts: a rifle, pistols, grenades. As for melee weapons, one carried a military knife—possibly more than one—while the other had equipped himself with a short, modern combat sword. All-round soldiers, with less firepower than the beefy tank[*](#0x00-fat) but more than the medic.

The most significant difference between the assault troopers was their rifles. One used an M16. The other held a gold-plated Kalashnikov encrusted with precious stones. The weapon glittered ominously in the light of their flashlights and muzzle flashes.

The players' weapons and equipment cost more than the real things. For the money they'd spent on skins[*](#0x00-skin) for their knives and rifles, they could have bought the same weapons in real life.

&mdash; What a kindergarten, — Alice thought aloud. But the Witch couldn't speak, so the game engine[*](#0x00-engine) translated Alice's voice into a long, mournful female howl.

The team below stopped.

&mdash; Hear that?

Alice crawled back into the darkness.

&mdash; Above us! Watch the rooftops. They'll be dropping on us any second!

&mdash; Witch on the roof!

&mdash; Take her out!

&mdash; See? And you said they were stupid. We're only getting started! — commented the man with the golden assault rifle. — Nice add-on[*](#0x00-addon)!

An unpleasant ache spread through her chest; a pang of grief and self-pity stabbed at her soul. Her brain had briefly performed its own death.

*And they've got night-vision goggles, too,* the girl sighed as she floated, invisible and inaudible, in spectator mode[*](#0x00-spectator) above her murdered body.

But the client had been identified, and that was what mattered.

The main fight would take place in the mansion. That much was obvious, and she'd almost certainly have to catch the client there. Better to improve her odds somehow and try to get close to him sooner—but what would happen if she took out one of the team? It would weaken the squad, sure, but what would they do? Wait for their comrade to respawn? He might not make it back alone. Go back for him? That would take time, but it was another possibility. Or would the client restart the game because they'd blown the attempt[*](#0x00-try)? Then both Alice and the Agency team would have to do everything all over again. A restart meant failure. She couldn't allow it.

Besides, she didn't know what rules they'd set for themselves, how they intended to play from here on, or what they would count as a loss.

Who couldn't she kill? Without the tank, they wouldn't go any farther. Same with the medic. The client was off limits, obviously. That left the other assault trooper.

Ideally, the client would reach the final bloodbath with the tank, and the two of them, high on adrenaline, would try to pull it off alone. After all, once you've made it to the boss, why the hell not?

&mdash; Come to think of it, we've all got our own final boss, — the girl concluded, bringing up the map on her internal display. Alice picked a new mob at random, a block away from her own corpse.

In her fleshy, sickly-colored man's hand, she found an adjustable wrench. Now that was something! At least she wasn't going in armed with nothing but a manicure. Small mercies!

She weighed the tool in her hand and was pleased.

*Heavy is good. Heavy is reliable!* she recalled the immortal line.

The gamer gave herself a quick once-over. She had landed in a solidly built male body with a rotten hole in its chest, mangled arms, and a long beard.

The girl turned around and grimaced. Behind her stood three more of the same decomposing beauties, gently swaying on their feet, wearing hard hats and overalls in different colors. Their pupils reflected a nasty white gleam, like those of animals, transforming ordinary dead meat into something otherworldly.

Her virtual-reality headset couldn't generate smells, but her brain knew what decomposing people smelled like, so it supplied the missing ingredient. Alice remembered the sickly-sweet reek of putrescine and cadaverine.

All the zombies were staring at her as though waiting for orders.

&mdash; Gentle-ghouls, do try to look a little less grim, — Alice suggested, hoping to suppress her revulsion. Only inarticulate, creaking noises came from her character's mouth.

She turned and took a couple of steps toward the approaching players when three equally disgusting creaks sounded behind her.

The whole situation was beginning to smell distinctly unlike chocolate.

Fighting a growing wave of dread, Alice slowly turned back toward the three gloomy beauties. The zombies were still swaying where they stood, their dead, unblinking gazes fixed on her.

Alice stared hard at the faces disfigured by the 3D sculptors. Some ancient part of her brain, honed over millions of years of evolution, was desperately sounding the alarm.

Of course, the developers might have given the mobs the ability to communicate and answer one another. Why shouldn't they call back and forth, creating background noise to build the atmosphere and frighten the players? These creatures were scripted[*](#0x00-scripted) entities with limited behavioral algorithms. Essentially mannequins that could move and attack.

Right now, Alice was supposed to be part of the scenery. Which meant the zombies shouldn't react to her. They shouldn't be looking at her. The girl stepped sideways, and three heads turned to follow her.

&mdash; Well, this evening's getting interesting, isn't it? — Alice whispered, trying to steady her ragged breathing.

The self-learning AI was beginning to suspect something. It could see a player in her, and very soon it might start hunting her, too.

Alice turned and ran toward the approaching quartet of shooting enthusiasts. Footsteps sounded behind her. Looking back, she saw all three zombies following, never taking their eyes off her.

&mdash; Mother of—! — Alice swore. They couldn't catch her, since identical characters had identical movement speeds. As long as she didn't make a mistake somewhere.

The girl burst out of the alley right in front of the players and, without slowing down, expecting a bullet in her side at any moment, plunged into the next one.

&mdash; Did you guys just see that? — the knight commented.

&mdash; Someone's had one too many, — another voice ventured, and the men all burst out laughing.

Between the rectangular houses ran straight little alleyways, randomly furnished with debris when the level was generated so that nobody could shoot straight through them. The rectangular routes allowed mobs to reach any point in the city in constant time, and they knew how to navigate around local obstacles.

The team passed by, and Alice continued running down the alley.

&mdash; The moment of truth, — the girl decided, standing in the middle of the street and feeling her heart trying to break her ribs from the inside. The air once again filled with the smells of decay and rot.

&mdash; Calm down. They're not real, — the girl whispered to herself, studying the dead people standing before her. — Just models, textures, and animation.

Alice looked around.

&mdash; The AI is aggroing, but it isn't attacking. You can see me, can't you? — she asked the empty air.

An uneven chorus of voices answered, communicating something at the level of the most primitive reflexes.

&mdash; And you don't know what to do yet, but you'll work it out soon enough. Okay!

The zombies howled, reacting to the volume of Alice's character's wail.

&mdash; The client wanted unconventional behavior? He's going to get it! I'll build the greatest mob train[*](#0x00-train) in the history of the world!

Alice set off at a run through the dark streets, pulling aggro[*](#0x00-agro) onto herself.

Dark streets gave way to heaps of rubbish, the bodies of the twice-dead left in the players' wake, wrecked cars, and doors torn from their hinges. In the darkness of broken windows, gateways, and buildings, the eyes of the undead glittered as their owners eagerly joined the procession. Her character had clearly never won any prizes for running when he was alive, so the other NPCs were barely falling behind. The Witch's body would certainly have helped here, but that was for dessert. In other words, after she died.

Alice surveyed her little army: crowds of ordinary dead people in various states of freshness, several mutated brutes, two Witches, an enormous fat man, and creatures that moved on all fours. The whole mass of them shuffled, moaned, grunted, and growled, awaiting orders.

&mdash; If necromancers existed, I imagine it'd look something like this, — she decided.

The fact that she'd managed to gather an army was pure luck. The NPCs could have been tied to their own locations[*](#0x00-location), but because the map was regenerated for every round, they'd been given freedom of movement.

Trying to corner the desperate foursome in a narrow street would destroy her army. She needed chaos, and there was just the place for it: the garden by the mansion. She had to enter the garden *behind* the players, to drive them toward the house rather than block their way. And after that, whatever happened, happened.

&mdash; Follow me, you filthy plague-bags! — Alice commanded. — We're going to have fun, but not everyone gets out alive!

Grenades and Molotov cocktails flew through the air, accompanied by some spectacular swearing from the players. Explosions tore apart and scattered some of the attackers, opening gaps in the flow, but the zombie tsunami kept rolling in, filling the garden. The cocktails, despite the damage they dealt to the undead, only made the assault more terrifying: burning dead people hurled themselves at the four men like screaming torches.

The other players' guns joined in, but Alice's army relentlessly pushed the brave foursome backward.

&mdash; They're coming in from the right!

&mdash; Told you they'd show us!

No great tragedy—the AI director would generously provide more ammo in the mansion to make the game more interesting. Alice always examined mirrors in games, because they were among the most important sources of truth. In reality, mirrors lied. Here, they didn't. Here, everything was the other way around.

Everything is always reversed on the other side of the mirror. Only mirrors can tell you, unequivocally, that you're inside a game.

Depending on the engine[*](#0x00-engine), a virtual world might calculate the paths of light rays, making a "real" mirror possible. But nobody ever did it a hundred percent. There wasn't enough computing power in the world to trace every ray, so mirrors like that were often cloudy, smeared, or broken to save resources. Huge or perfectly clear mirrors, when they were important to the decor or the plot, were always some kind of trick, sleight of hand, deception. Developers had amassed a whole wagonload and then some of ways to convince players that a reflection was real.

Alice hurried away, careful not to stare into the mirror too long and start seeing things she would rather not see.

Footsteps and the clatter of weapons sounded on the first floor. Several gunshots put an end to someone's nonexistence, and the footsteps began to recede. A broad concrete staircase with rails led down into the basement.

From time to time, ordinary zombies ran out of several passages leading down into the dungeon, distracting the players from the boss. The monster also snacked on them to restore its health. Each wave brought more mobs; killing them became harder and harder, so there was no time to waste.

There were three ways to defeat this fat bastard—fat in every sense of the word: shoot it, burn it, or combine the two.

Option one was to dance around the monster for ages, burning through a mountain of nerves and ammunition while dodging attacks that grew increasingly elaborate and desperate as the boss's health dwindled. Pulling off that trick with only two players would be unlikely. They wouldn't have enough ammo.

Option two was to sever the cables holding barrels of gasoline in towers that the game designers had thoughtfully stacked in the corners of the rectangular arena. According to the backstory, of course, a previous group had planned the whole thing but failed to pull it off. The fuel had been delivered, but there'd been nobody left to set it alight. To make things easier, you could simply circle around one of the towers and wait for the boss to strike at you. The blow would snap the cables and send the barrels rolling across the arena. To win, you needed to bring down all four towers, ignite any one barrel, and run for it. But the more barrels ended up on the ground, the more carefully you had to shoot at the advancing hordes of helper mobs, lest you set the whole thing off too early.

And the third option was to shoot a tower while the boss was beside it. The resulting detonation wouldn't kill the monster, but it would take a substantial chunk out of its health. Repeat four times for a complete victory.

The players had settled on the second option.

The knight danced around the monster, keeping its attention on himself and luring it toward the corner farthest from the entrance so he could bait it into striking and destroy one of the barrel towers. At the opposite end of the arena, the client was busy shooting the cables where they were anchored to the floor and walls.

The girl stepped back into the passage to wait for the next wave of zombies.

The game was over. Work had begun.

Alice drew a deep breath and tried to calm her racing heart. The time had come. The hour had struck. The moment was upon her. Thinking of time, Alice conjured up the image of a plain office clock: tick marks instead of numbers, black rectangular hands, and a thin red second hand, fine as a line, moving in little jerks around the dial.

This world was not real. It was only a representation of information. Only pictures. There was no matter here, no air, no earth, no fire, no water, no life and no death. Only time was real here. Computing time. That which had no computing time could not exist.

The girl felt the virtual world around her begin to swim, losing its depth.

There was only time here, and that time was the time of reality. She had time—Alice had time. And so did he: the player with the golden assault rifle.

His image was a costume, and beneath it was a real human being who breathed, whose heart — his personal timer—beat, counting off the time that passed.

Alice felt her hands grow heavy. Before her mind's eye, the second hand slowed rapidly, coming to a stop.

Behind her came a howl, and a crowd of generated monsters rushed down the stairs from the first floor. Alice stepped out of the passage and pressed her back against the wall beside the entrance.

The hand stopped. From this moment on, that which had no computing time did not exist.

Running up to the thinning crowd pressing in on the player, Alice swept her claws through them, forcing a path. An NPC caught by the blow froze and dissolved into thin air. Another swipe, and another character vanished.

A devastating blow shook the mansion. Alice suddenly realized that the knight had failed to dodge.

The next instant, she lunged at the player herself.

Her Witch's claws passed through the character, but Alice felt that she truly possessed those claws, and that they were truly touching something alive, something real. Passing through some kind of warm mist, they tore threads as fine as cobwebs. Something defenseless snapped with a ringing no ear could hear.

The player jammed the barrel of his rifle into her stomach and pulled the trigger. Alice felt nonexistent bullets tear apart nonexistent organs and shatter a nonexistent spine.

The game engine calculated the damage. Thrown backward by the propellant gases, the Witch folded in half and lay on the floor like a filthy rag. Alice was expelled from the body.

The player began to thrash about. His character jerked and dropped the rifle. Before Alice's mind's eye stood the office clock. Not one of its hands would ever move again. The clock was dead.

The boss struck with its hideous tentacle, knocking away half the health of the still-living character of an already-dead man.

Alice thought that she wanted out. Every diver had their own method: some recited little poems to slip into an altered state of consciousness, some recalled a moment of extreme stress, and some, perhaps, prayed.

Alice strained to break free as though from sleep paralysis, pouring all her desire into leaving this place immediately, willing herself away and tearing the fabric of space with her determination.

Space crackled and gave way.

The magic of virtual reality's hypnosis fell apart. The living world flattened into a picture on the screen of her VR headset.

On that screen, the boss landed another blow on the player's motionless character. The character died. The round ended.

Alice severed the connection to the server, switched off her computer, removed her headset, and pulled off her gloves. She looked down at her hand, where the sensation of touching the soft mist still lingered.

The job was done. Fragments, scraps, and shards of everything she had seen, heard, and done whirled through her head like a hurricane. Snatches of the players' conversations, the client's dossier, pieces of text from hundreds of pages of manuals on monsters and game mechanics. To operate effectively, you had to know the peculiarities of mobs and bosses and remember their stats: health, damage, movement speed, behavior strategies, attack timings. You had to know the players' character stats, understand the game's arsenal, remember weapon specifications, the ins and outs of level generation, the quirks of the engine, known bugs, and on and on and on. As with any profession, it took many hours of theory and practice.

The cat came running, crying plaintively, and headbutted Alice.

&mdash; In a minute, sweetheart. I'll feed you in a minute, — she replied wearily.

Alice unplugged the wires and trudged to the kitchen. Brown little pebbles rattled against the bottom of a metal food bowl.

&mdash; This stuff drives you crazy, doesn't it? — the girl smiled. The cat approved the serving with a delighted meow.

Peeling off her VR suit[*](#0x00-vrsuit), Alice tossed it into the bathtub and carefully inspected herself and her surroundings in the large mirror. Finding no artifacts, she poked the mirror with a finger, climbed into the tub, and stood on the suit. From the kitchen came the crunch of the cat chewing his food. She still had to clean the litter box for that little Purr-chacho, but that could wait.

Right now, she needed to wash it all off. Even the things that couldn't be washed away.
