&mdash; Li-ittle one, come ou-ut, &mdash; the girl drawled sweetly, stretching the vowels as she wandered slowly between the beds, peering under each one in turn. She already knew where her fugitive was hiding, but right now she wanted to play.

&mdash; Where are you? Why did you run away?

Aera straightened up and stretched, tired. Hide-and-seek was over. The pre-sunset September sun looked in through the windows, stamping golden rectangles onto the rows of children's cots.

&mdash; I'll find you anyway! &mdash; she told the silence with a smile, but no one answered.

Taking a careful step, the girl peeked under the nearest bed again. In the empty children's camp the beds were still made.

&mdash; Not here either… &mdash; sighed the [*it*](#it) and turned around &mdash; her partner had appeared in the doorway. Aera put a finger to her lips, asking her not to break the moment of the game.

&mdash; Little one, I know where you are. Show yourself &mdash; you're a man!

She approached the cot under which the boy was hiding, slowly. With every step dust rose into the air, flaring into hundreds of bright points in the sunbeams.

&mdash; You're a big boy now. Come out! &mdash; the girl demanded sternly. &mdash; Don't make me drag you out.

Aera stopped beside the bed so he couldn't be reached by hand.

&mdash; Acting like a little kid, &mdash; she said gently, looking under the cot.

&mdash; A-a-auntie, p-p-please, don't! &mdash; the youth squeaked in a thin, tearful voice, holding his hands out in front of him.

Aera straightened up, glanced at the pistol in her hand, and sighed:

&mdash; Oh, drop it. It's pointless.

Without looking, she shoved the barrel under the bed and squeezed the trigger.

The shot cracked in her ears, cutting off the frightened squeal. The cot bolted to the floor jerked, and everything went quiet.

&mdash; There. The last one, &mdash; the fighter commented, pressing her monstrous-looking pistol to her right shin. With a light click the weapon magnetized back into its holster.

&mdash; You're really… &mdash; Sveta didn't finish.

&mdash; Where else am I supposed to put him? What am I supposed to do with him? When it's banditry he's a grown man, but when it's dying it's 'auntie, please don't!' &mdash; Aera mocked the boy's last words.

Leaving the dormitory, she looked back at the neat rows of cots. It was strange that in the looted buildings the children's beds still had linens on them. Apparently they were still very radioactive.

&mdash; He needs to be burned, &mdash; Svetlana reminded her.

Aera had pulled her out of the clutches of the Awakened a week ago. A typical lab rat &mdash; thin, tall, half a head taller, with fine features, glasses, and hair forever unkempt. A scientist, obvious at first glance.

&mdash; I've still got five hours.

&mdash; Better do it now. It'll get dark soon, and coming back here won't be pleasant.

Aera grimaced unhappily and returned to the dormitory. She had to crawl under the cot to pull out the still-warm corpse. The fighter took the body by the arm and dragged it behind her, leaving a brown trail on the dusty floor.

&mdash; Help, &mdash; she threw over her shoulder to Svetlana. Sveta took the other wrist, and the girls hauled the body down the stairs littered with trash, crumbled wall plaster, and shards of glass.

&mdash; When else will I get to walk hand in hand with a boy? &mdash; Aera grumbled.

&mdash; Don't even say it, &mdash; her partner echoed. She didn't feel like talking after the last performance. Aera was far from always behaving like a human being. But if you live with wolves, you howl like a wolf &mdash; Sveta had decided.

The girls dragged the body into the inner courtyard, surrounded on three sides by two-story panel-block buildings. Every structure built in the standard Cyrillic П shape gaped with black holes where windows had been knocked out.

&mdash; Get over by the wall, &mdash; Aera ordered her and crouched beside the corpse.

She reached into her pocket and stopped, catching the attentive gaze of the dead boy's bright blue eyes.

&mdash; Fuck… &mdash; the girl whispered.

&mdash; You didn't have to kill him. Take him with you, get him to the Fortress. He slipped, but he hadn't really gotten dirty yet.

The corpse watched Aera with its eyes. Its lips didn't move, but she could still hear the voice. She knew its eyes were actually closed. Aera reminded herself this was a hallucination, and it couldn't physically harm her.

&mdash; Are you saying something? &mdash; Svetlana asked, obediently standing by the wall beneath the black gap of a window.

&mdash; Swearing. Nothing, &mdash; the fighter answered briefly.

She had to shut him up fast, before he prophesied something again. Aera stood and looked around for something to smash the talker's head with.

&mdash; You won't save the old world.

Her gaze stopped on a large chunk of concrete, weighing maybe twenty kilograms. Apparently it had once been part of a wall. Flushing from the effort, the girl lifted the stone above her head and swung.

&mdash; What are you doing? &mdash; Svetlana worried.

&mdash; From the old that has lost its form, the new is born…

Aera brought the chunk down with all her force on the chatty corpse's head. There was a wet crunch, and thick dark fluid splashed in every direction.

&mdash; Why did you… &mdash; Svetlana couldn't find words. &mdash; That was cruel!

&mdash; Bullets are precious! The rest need to be done the same way.

&mdash; With stones?

&mdash; Could use a shovel, &mdash; Aera calmed down. &mdash; Or a crowbar, if we find one.

The girls circled the buildings looking for anything that might be useful. But their main haul waited in the director's office and the sizable reception room before it, which the destroyed gang of bandits had turned into living quarters, welding bars onto the windows. Probably the metal doors separating those two rooms from the corridors had helped too.

Aera checked the locks on the door and leaned out between the bars up to her waist. To Svetlana this seemed eccentric.

&mdash; Canned meat, rags, trinkets, gasoline, ammo, &mdash; Aera commented on the finds aloud. She scooped the women's jewelry she'd found into the first rag that came to hand, which turned out to be a handkerchief, and held out the little bundle to Svetlana.

&mdash; I won't take it, &mdash; the lab assistant refused.

&mdash; Are you stupid? In our time gold and bullets are the only currency.

&mdash; It's probably those girls' from the basement.

&mdash; And not only theirs, &mdash; Aera cut her off. &mdash; They won't need it anymore. I tossed in two grenades. It's mincemeat down there.

&mdash; And I still don't get why you killed them, &mdash; Sveta asked, still keeping her distance from the handkerchief of gold.

Aera lowered her hand, her whole manner showing she was tired of the other's foolishness.

&mdash; They were used as muscle. Tortured. Raped. You think the garden beds we walked through were tended by the bandits? They won't survive on their own, and we can't drag them along. Leaving them here means condemning them to an even more horrible death. I spared them suffering. I didn't kill them &mdash; I gave them death.

Aera held out the handkerchief again:

&mdash; Take it. It'll come in handy at the Fortress.

Sveta took the dirty bundle and put it in her pocket, zipping it shut.

&mdash; Blood gold, &mdash; she commented.

&mdash; I don't have any other kind for you. Gather food, ammo, rags. Leave the canisters here for now. Let's collect everything useful, then decapitation.

Aera realized she was looking at the stars. She had woken suddenly and at once. That always meant only one thing &mdash; deadly danger nearby.

Without moving, the girl listened. Wind rustled the leaves of a nearby forest and howled through the empty corridors of the ruined buildings. Ten meters away, at the other edge of the roof, Svetlana breathed heavily in her sleep. The nearly full Moon &mdash; beacon of the dead &mdash; flooded the space around them with white light. And something else was present, something elusive, introducing dissonance into the picture of nocturnal calm.

The wind died down, and Aera heard wet chewing. A group of unknown creatures was tearing chunks of flesh from the boy's body they'd left below, steadily, unhurriedly working their jaws. It didn't sound like an animal feast, with the usual snuffling, growling, and scuffling. That's how people eat when they're thoughtful about it.

As smoothly and carefully as she could, Aera crawled to Svetlana and clamped a hand over her mouth. Startled, her companion woke, thrashed, and stared at her friend with wild eyes. Aera gestured for silence.

&mdash; Don't move. Listen, &mdash; she whispered in Sveta's ear and removed her hand.

Tension and confusion reflected in the lab assistant's eyes, but when the chewing reached her too, her look turned to fear.

Aera reminded her again to stay silent and crawled back to her edge of the roof.

Feral cannibals? The corpse was still relatively fresh, the meat young &mdash; why not?

The girl cautiously looked over the edge.

The creature devouring the body looked like a spider assembled from human remains. Seven and a half mutilated legs &mdash; one foot was missing &mdash; stood firmly on the ground. Two human heads methodically stripped meat from the youth's legs, holding them with six human hands. Two human spines ran along a muscular back.

The chewing stopped. The creature pulled away from its meal and went on alert.

Aera ducked back and exhaled in bursts.

New form. New type of monster.

The girl looked at Svetlana. She was staring at Aera's face with hope and horror. Aera drew her thumb across her throat. Svetlana shut her eyes as hard as she could and curled into a ball.

Down there sat something that her invincible companion had acknowledged as death.

Aera heard with relief that the chewing resumed, though now less frequently.

Creeping up to the lab assistant, she whispered:

&mdash; Are you afraid of spiders?

&mdash; Don't especially like them.

&mdash; Then you're really not going to like this one.

&mdash; What's down there?

&mdash; Remember the slave girls?

&mdash; I never saw them.

&mdash; Well… it's assembled from their bodies.

Confusion, realization, and horror crossed Svetlana's face, but to the last was unexpectedly mixed the curiosity of a researcher.

&mdash; Do we wait for morning?

&mdash; I don't know how fast or tough it is. Or whether it can climb walls. Several stairways lead up to our roof, the thing has at least two heads &mdash; they'll figure it out. Besides, those girls probably know the compound better than we do.

&mdash; What do we do? &mdash; Svetlana asked hopefully.

&mdash; We'll use the ancient method of auto-da-fé &mdash; two canisters in the office. Lure it in there, lock it up, and burn it. Do we have the keys?

&mdash; We do.

Before Aera insisted on meeting the dawn on the roof, Svetlana had wanted to barricade herself in a utility room. But then there would be nowhere to retreat, and the Awakened don't climb walls or complex stairways. In a corridor, though, they can perfectly well wait under a door.

&mdash; Here's the plan. I crawl to the office and spread gasoline there. Then I make noise and lead it after me. When it runs inside, you slam the door and turn the key. The door's four millimeters thick, solid, the lock's decent too. Just lock it in time, and I'll set the fire.

&mdash; I don't like it, &mdash; Sveta answered.

&mdash; Me neither, but we have to deal with this one way or another.

&mdash; Maybe we wait for morning?

&mdash; So it can get a better look at us in broad daylight? Do you know what it's capable of?

&mdash; No.

&mdash; Neither do I. And I don't want to find out. Try not to die. And don't scream when you see it. There's plenty to scream about.


Crawling to the block door leading from the roof to the second floor turned out to be simple. But now she had to open the leather-clad wooden door without a sound, descend the rungs of the metal staircase, cross an entire building through the corridor, and spill gasoline on the floor of the director's office. All of it without a single sound.

Aera crouched beside the door, pressed her fingertips against it, and slowly, millimeter by millimeter, began to open it. She remembered perfectly how piercingly the long-rusted hinges had squeaked when the girls shut the door behind them before spending the night.

Holding her breath, bit by bit, the fighter opened the old door.

The wind helped a little, periodically raising a howl on the empty floors, but the same wind could become an enemy at any moment, carrying her living scent to the dead monster.

Holding the open door, Aera slowly, softly, step by step, descended the staircase to the second floor and crouched, listening. Below, the creature was casually chewing human flesh and humming children's songs. Apparently the dead slave girls were finally getting their fill.

On all fours, leaning on her hands, Aera slowly crawled along the corridor.

If only Sveta's nerves held. If only she didn't come out too soon.

Aera carefully chose spots for her feet and hands, smoothly moving herself another half meter forward. And another half meter. Don't breathe. Freeze, listen. Another half meter.

The even surface of the concrete floor exploded deafeningly under her hand. It turned out to be evenly covered with glass dust.

&mdash; Fuck, &mdash; Aera whispered.

&mdash; Mama! &mdash; the monster shrieked in two voices.

In the silence that followed, it seemed her heart was ready to leap from her chest.

She felt both heads turn toward her. The monster sniffed and lunged toward the source of the sound. Dry grass rustled, glass crunched, wooden window frames creaked.

Aera ran for the director's office with everything she had. Seconds counted.

&mdash; Run! &mdash; Svetlana already shouted after her.

Single shots rang out from an automatic rifle. Several bullets ricocheted and whistled down the corridor of the lower floor. Aera reached the door and tried to insert the key into the lock with shaking hands. The key, as if alive, jumped from her hand and clattered to the floor.

&mdash; Bitch, bitch, bitch! &mdash; Aera screamed, grabbed it, and forcing the door, drove it into the keyhole. In the window opening a pair of legs appeared, three hands gripped the frame, and a head looked into the corridor.

Aera heard bullets thud dully against concrete walls. The head in the window jerked unexpectedly and, wailing long and loud, the monster vanished. Apparently Svetlana had hit it after all.

The fighter burst into the reception room, knocked over the only chair, flung open the office door, and grabbed a canister. Every delay seemed to stretch into long, agonizing minutes. Unscrewing the cap, the girl splashed gasoline on the bookcase, desk, a couple of cots, and threw the canister on the floor, from which fragrant clear liquid poured out. She opened the second canister and tossed it into the middle of the reception room.

Outside, Sveta screamed. Single shots grew into short bursts.

Aera dashed for the exit.

The spider was climbing toward Svetlana on the roof, awkwardly catching on empty window frames. Svetlana, panicking, fired in front of her.

Aera whipped out her pistol and took aim.

The spider, gripping the front edge with all six human hands, pulled itself up and looked at the retreating girl holding an automatic rifle at arm's length in front of her.

Aera fired. One of the spider's hands spun through the air and fell onto the backpacks of supplies. The spider shrieked and turned around.

&mdash; Hey, you piece of shit, I'm here! &mdash; the fighter shouted. &mdash; I'm more dangerous! I'm here!

The girl aimed again, wanting after all to hit one of the heads, but the new bullet went somewhere into the leg area. The spider lost its grip and, collecting the remaining glass in the window frames as it fell, crashed to the ground. A piercing girlish scream filled the area; shards of glass rang as they scattered.

The monster stopped writhing, leaped up, and, howling in a human voice, charged at her.

&mdash; Come on! &mdash; Aera shouted. The cry snapped Sveta out of her stupor and she ran toward the anteroom that half an hour ago Aera had had to crawl to.

The monster, sobbing, clambered over the second-floor window and rushed at Aera. The girl darted into the room and, reaching the window, grabbed the bars. Misjudging its speed, the monster slammed into the iron door, but with its remaining five hands caught the frame and hurled itself inside.

Aera tore through the bars, jumped out the window, but didn't manage to spring clear. Five hands grabbed her leg and dragged her inside.

&mdash; Sveta, hurry! &mdash; the girl screamed, gripped an iron bar, and kicked the nearest woman's hand with her free leg. Both heads emerged from the darkness, and the nearest immediately sank its teeth into her calf. Aera screamed in pain and remembered the pistol. Letting go of the bar with one hand, she tore it from the magnetic holster, jammed the barrel into the monster's head, and pulled the trigger.

All the creature's hands holding the girl went slack and the spider recoiled.

The iron door slammed. Svetlana pressed it with her whole body and turned the key. The lock clicked once, twice.

&mdash; Down! &mdash; Aera shouted.

Her leg hurt brutally, but the spider hadn't bitten through her uniform fabric. Aera pulled herself up, hauled herself through the bars, and met the gaze of yet another pair of eyes.

The girl pushed off and flew backward. The monster crashed into the metal bars; five hands clawed at the air where she had just been.

The impact with the ground knocked the wind out of her. Aera hit her head hard.

The spider, howling, tore at the bars, but realizing it couldn't get through, ran back inside. Blows sounded against the iron door.

&mdash; Good girl, &mdash; Aera muttered.

She raised the pistol, aimed with a blurry gaze at an iron bar, and fired. The bullet grazed the metal but produced no sparks.

&mdash; Too damp, maybe…

Svetlana leaned out from the window of the second floor of the neighboring room. Quickly scanning the darkness of the likely landing spot, she jumped to the ground, fell, scrambled up, and, staggering, ran to her friend.

&mdash; Lighter! &mdash; Aera shouted to her. &mdash; You've got one! Faster!

Her shaking hands wouldn't obey; with disobedient fingers Sveta searched her pockets.

&mdash; Right pocket, &mdash; Aera hinted, wincing from pain.

After fumbling, Sveta pulled out a gasoline lighter.

The monster could break down the door now. In that carcass there were three hundred kilograms of human meat, or five or six average-sized girls. So everything had to work the first time. And for it to work, she had to calm down.

&mdash; That's it, &mdash; Aera said in a tone unexpectedly calm even to herself. &mdash; Calm down. Light it and throw it through the window.

Sveta obeyed. The burning lighter flew through the window. An explosion thundered, and flames roared out of the windows, lighting the entire surrounding area. A hot wave knocked Svetlana off her feet. The spider shrieked in the fire.

&mdash; Burn, burn bright, don't go out! &mdash; Aera shouted with vicious glee. Svetlana dragged her grinning friend farther from the wall and the fire.

&mdash; Mama, mommy! &mdash; the spider shrieked, thrashing in the flames. The monster threw itself at the window bars, pushed an entire head between them, and reached toward the girls with scorched hands.

&mdash; Help! &mdash; the monster cried in a pleading voice.

Aera turned away.

&mdash; Heeelp!!!

Sveta was shaking. She turned her back to the fire, closed her eyes, and covered her ears. The air filled with the bitter smell of burnt meat.

&mdash; It hurts! Mama! Help! It hurts! Help! Mama!

The charring monster shrieked and battered against the red-hot bars, alternately pushing multiple arms and legs through them.

Aera, swaying, stood and raised the pistol. A shot thundered. The monster's second head burst, cutting off the deafening cries. Only the flames kept roaring, devouring the bandits' crude furnishings.

Svetlana sobbed and wiped her tears, smearing dirt across her face.

&mdash; They died back then, so you understand, &mdash; Aera broke the silence. &mdash; And this, &mdash; she nodded toward the charring corpse, &mdash; is fragments of memory preserved in the brains of the heads.

&mdash; I know, &mdash; Sveta whispered.

Aera sat tiredly on the ground, hugged her knees, and tilted her head back, looking at the sky. Svetlana sank down beside her and looked up too.

Dawn was beginning.
